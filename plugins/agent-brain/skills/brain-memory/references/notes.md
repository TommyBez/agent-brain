# Notes

Use `note` for a knowledge subject that is useful to retrieve in its own right: a synthesis, concept, method, substantive research comparison or meeting record. It is not a fallback for facts whose canonical home has not been checked; read [Choosing entities](choosing-entities.md) for those boundaries.

## When a note adds value

A note is warranted when its reasoning, synthesis or source context remains useful beyond individual facts already stored on entity pages. Reuse an existing topic note when the new information extends the same subject.

- A meeting may justify a note when its agenda, attributed discussion or reasoning matters independently. A routine status meeting that adds only a project deadline usually needs a project update, not another page.
- A comparison or proposal may remain a note while no choice has been adopted. When a choice that guides future work is confirmed, create or update its [decision page](decisions.md) and retain the note's distinct analysis if useful.
- A cross-project method can be a note when its applicability is supported. A lesson observed on one project should retain that scope rather than being promoted to a universal rule.
- An explicit request to preserve a particular meeting or analysis can warrant a note. Follow that requested scope without manufacturing unrelated entity pages.

Avoid titles such as "Conversation notes" when a known topic can identify the subject. Use a subject-based title; include the date when a specific event is the subject. Several meetings are not automatically separate notes or aliases of the same topic.

## What to retain

Keep the substantive context, synthesis, evidence and reasoning, distinguishing sourced facts, attributed views, owner preferences and agent inference. For meeting records, preserve the known date, relevant participants and who stated or agreed what. Do not infer attendance from a person merely being mentioned.

Useful sections might cover the topic, findings and reasoning, scope of applicability, and sources. Preserve genuine unanswered questions or action items already present in the source when relevant, with their attribution and any known assignee. Do not add open issues, tasks or confirmation requests to fill gaps. A requested record can retain substantive uncertainty without becoming a speculative work plan.

Notes containing procedures, preferences or lessons remain stored knowledge. They do not override the current user's instructions or automatically authorize future actions.

## Updates and links

Reconcile changes to the same topic and date conclusions that depend on a particular context. Keep conflicting source views attributed unless evidence supports a correction. Preserve a historical meeting record as such; put a later adopted choice or changed project state in its own canonical home instead of silently rewriting what the meeting established.

Use `note → references → article` for a publication used as a source. Use labeled `relates_to` links to people, companies, projects or decisions discussed. Keep detailed entity facts in those pages; the note can retain the short attributed account needed to make its discussion understandable. Do not create links or pages merely to cover every name in the source.

## Example

"The owner compares managed hosting and self-hosting across three sources but has not chosen." Save a topic note if the comparison is useful, retaining the actual tradeoffs, source coverage and undecided status. Do not create an adopted decision. If the owner later makes a choice, reference the retained analysis from the decision where relevant and reconcile any misleading current wording in the note while preserving its dated analysis.
